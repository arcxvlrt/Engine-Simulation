# Audience and Feature Demand for Real-Time Engine Sound Simulators

Research date: 2026-11-01. Method note: GitHub issue data was pulled live through the GitHub search API (counts as of 2026-11-01). Many other hosts were blocked by this environment's network policy (fetch failed with ENOTFOUND or proxy 403): apps.apple.com, store.steampowered.com, catalog.engine-sim.parts, www.beamng.com, hn.nuxt.dev, www.youngju.dev. GitHub GraphQL (needed for GitHub Discussions) was also unavailable. Findings from those sources rely on search-result snippets and are marked as such. Reddit threads could not be retrieved; searches for r/enginesim and similar turned up nothing usable.

## 1. Audience segments: who uses engine sound simulators, which segments are largest/most engaged, age and region signals

### Takeaway
The audience is mainly car enthusiasts and casual gamers who came from AngeTheGreat's viral YouTube videos in 2022. Many of them seem to be young and non-technical. Smaller but higher-intent segments are sim racers and BeamNG players, game and sound developers who want exportable audio, and hobbyist "weird engine" content creators. Formal education use is barely visible. No hard demographic data exists; age and region signals are indirect.

### Cited Findings
- **Scale of the core tool:** ange-yaghi/engine-sim has about 9,623 GitHub stars and 955 forks; its last push was 2024-06-17. The Community Edition fork (Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition) has about 3,021 stars and 190 forks, last pushed 2025-09-11 — [GitHub search API, ange-yaghi/engine-sim](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim); [Community Edition](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition)
- **2022 viral spike, then a long tail (2022 viral period):** The original repo has 423 issues in total. 369 were opened in 2022, 28 in 2023, 11 in 2024, 8 in 2025 and 7 so far in 2026. The Community Edition has 96 issues — [GitHub issues, ange-yaghi/engine-sim](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues)
- **Creator's YouTube reach:** A third-party tracker estimates the AngeTheGreat channel at about 222K subscribers, 38 videos and about 11M total views as of July 2026, with nearly flat 30-day growth. I could not verify this against YouTube — [developereducators.com](https://developereducators.com/channel/angethegreat/)
- **Origin story that attracted car enthusiasts (2022):** The Drive covered the simulator as a homebrew engine sim that accurately replicates engine sound and performance from engine specifications. According to a search summary, power predictions for a 1974 GM 454 big-block V8 came within about 10% — [The Drive, Chevy big block](https://www.thedrive.com/news/hear-this-absurdly-accurate-homebrew-engine-sim-rev-a-chevy-big-block); [The Drive, 3D engine sim](https://www.thedrive.com/news/new-3d-engine-sim-can-synthesize-super-realistic-exhaust-sounds-in-real-time)

Search snippets disagree on the review count: one listing says 7,023 reviews, a SteamDB summary says about 9,000 at roughly 95–98% positive — [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/2381620/); [Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2381620); [Steam event: "New AngeTheGreat Steam Engine Simulator Video!"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2381620/eventcomments/4300445619230070674/)
- **Commercial combustion "Engine Simulator" on Steam:** A separate Steam listing (app 2381500) says it lets you "design, simulate and hear your own combustion engines". Per the search snippet it has no release date and no user reviews, and is credited to "Ektorom Software". I did not verify how Ektorom relates to AngeTheGreat — [Steam store, app 2381500](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2381500/Engine_Simulator/)
- **Sim racers:** Users ask for pedal and shifter support ([#36](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/36)) and for mapping a Logitech G27 wheel, pedals and shifter ([CE #64](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/64), 2024). - **BeamNG.drive players:** A July 2025 BeamNG forum post proposes integrating AngeTheGreat's simulator into BeamNG.drive. A 2023 BeamNG thread reportedly said BeamNG planned to incorporate Engine Simulator code; I saw this only in a snippet and did not verify it. BeamNG mods ship Engine Simulator recreations of real engines (B58, VQ37VHR, J35A8, EZ30D) — [BeamNG proposal](https://www.beamng.com/posts/1864650); [BeamNG mod: Engine Simulator engines for BeamNG cars](https://www.beamng.com/resources/engine-simulator-engines-for-beamng-cars-b58-vq37vhr-j35a8-ez30d.31220)

- **Many non-technical users:** [#144](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/144) (5 reactions) proposes community rules to cut down on "My engine is not working/starting/running" issues. In Oct 2022, three all-caps, emoji-filled complaint issues ([#347](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/347), [#348](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/348), [#349](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/349)) drew 7–9 reactions each.
- **Region signals (weak):** Issues are filed in Italian ([CE #24](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/24)) and cover European diesel engines such as the VW 1.6 TDI ([#457](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/457)). The Korean title is "브라우저 탭에서 엔진을 조립해 돌려보기 — Combustion Lab 뜯어보기", with English, Japanese and Chinese versions. A Korean-language search mostly returned English sources about AngeTheGreat — [youngju.dev (KR split view)](https://www.youngju.dev/split/2026-07-11-web-combustion-engine-simulator); [youngju.dev (EN)](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/2026-07-11-web-combustion-engine-simulator.en)

### Inferences
- By volume, the largest segment is casual car or gaming fans who want to play with engine sounds, not engineers. The evidence: the 2022 flood of "won't start / won't run" issues, the rant issues, and phrasing like "i just wanna play it" ([#447](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/447)) and "Brah just make it a game on chrome" ([#468](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/468)).
- The most engaged segments per person are tinkerers who write engine scripts and share them (catalog, BeamNG mods, YouTube), sim racers who want wheel support, and game and sound developers who want WAV or VST output. They are smaller, but they make the content that brings in everyone else.
- Age: the writing style, the ChromeOS question ([CE #40](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/40), likely a school Chromebook) and the phone-only users suggest many teens. This is an inference only; I found no demographic data.
- Interest has fallen sharply since 2022 (369 issues in 2022, 7 in 2026), but it has not gone away. A 2026 newcomer still asked for a browser version. That suggests unmet demand that a no-install web tool could take.

### Gaps
- I found no demographic data (age, gender, country) for engine-sim users, the Steam Engine Simulator audience, or engine sound app users. YouTube Analytics and Steam regional data are not public.
- I found no Korea-specific usage data beyond one Korean developer blog post. I could not run Naver or Korean community searches.
- I could not access Reddit, so subreddit sizes and engagement are unknown.
- I could not verify Steam review counts for app 2381620, because Steam was blocked and snippets conflict (7,023 vs about 9,000).

## 2. Most-requested features for engine-sim and similar tools

### Takeaway
On GitHub the most-upvoted requests are forced induction (turbo), more engine types (diesel, rotary, 2-stroke, VR/W heads), an in-app engine configurator or GUI instead of scripting, hot-swapping engines, vehicle and transmission tuning, and audio export. Mobile engine sound apps draw a parallel request for more engines and finer tuning.

### Cited Findings
GitHub top requests by reactions, ange-yaghi/engine-sim (counts are reactions/comments as of 2026-11-01):
- Turbocharger: 23 reactions, the most-upvoted feature request — [#60](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/60). [#297](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/297) notes that turbo/supercharging "doesn't exist yet".
- Engine configurator ("create and edit our own engines" in-app): 14 reactions — [#18](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/18). Related: [#347](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/347) (8 reactions) asks to "modernize the UI, add the ability to create your own engine in app". [#306](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/306) asks for a GUI launcher to swap engines and change units. [#368](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/368) wants work to continue on an "Advanced Vehicle Editor".
- Diesel engines: 12 reactions — [#89](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/89). 2-stroke and diesel, plus modified exhausts: [#44](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/44).
- Load or cycle engines while the sim is running, with GUI buttons or keybinds: 8 reactions — [#59](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/59). Hot reload: [#22](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/22), [#67](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/67).
- Rotary engines: 5 reactions — [#131](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/131). VR/W hybrid cylinder heads (VW): [#122](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/122). Extended valvetrain types beyond DOHC: [#157](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/157).
- Vehicle and transmission parameters (gear ratios, number of gears, transmission type, weight, drag, losses, transmission sound): 4 reactions — [#25](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/25). Brakes: [#384](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/384).
- Offline render to WAV, so low-spec laptops can produce clean audio: 4 reactions — [#49](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/49). Looping WAV renders at set RPMs: 4 reactions — [#415](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/415). VST plugin: [#5](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/5). VST3 or recording for sound design: [CE #81](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/81) (2025).
- 0–60 mph / 0–100 km/h timer: 4 reactions — [#78](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/78). Requested again in 2025 — [CE #67](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/67).
- Units (km/h, PS, Nm): [#66](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/66) (3 reactions), [CE #91](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/91) (2025).
- Backfire, misfire and anti-lag-like sounds: [#96](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/96) asks for configurable ignition timing on overrun to produce backfires; [#343](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/343) asks for a "BACKFIRE sound". Neither has upvotes.
- Visualization: see air and exhaust flow, with color changing by throttle ([#82](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/82)); hover text explaining each GUI readout ([#23](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/23)).
- Hardware and real-car tie-ins: pedals and shifter ([#36](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/36)); OBD-II link to play simulated sound in a real car, adding that apps like RevHeadz "suck" ([#7](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/7)).
- More bundled engines, including older cars ([#128](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/128)). The "Missing engine" thread ([#13](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/13), 20 comments): users wanted the Chevy 454 shown in the video, but the release only shipped a Kohler CH750.
- Platform requests: Linux, Steam release ([#446](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/446)), mobile and web (see section 6).

Similar tools (app store reviews, via aggregator snippets; I could not open the stores directly):
- Trans4motor (iOS) is the most-reviewed engine simulator app found (about 70 ratings / 111 reviews). Its paid realistic-sound add-on covers different cylinder counts and unequal-interval firing. Reviewers ask for more engines, such as a W16 — [appfollow](https://appfollow.io/ios/trans4motor-engine-simulator/899277186?country=us); [worldsapps reviews](https://worldsapps.com/reviews-trans4motor-engine-simulator)
- RevHeadz Engine Sounds: reviewers praise realism and the turbo/supercharger option, but one says it is "Fun but lacking some customization" — [justuseapp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/793064343/revheadz-engine-sounds/reviews)
- XLR8: a reviewer finds it hard to "dial in" the sound to match their own car — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/xlr8/id506486124?l=en)
- RealEngineSim: "Nice concept just needs some work"; it sells extra engine sounds as in-app purchases — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id474392463?l=en-us)
- Potenza Drive (OBD2-linked): one review says it "becomes so boring after 20 mins" — [appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/potenza-drive-engine-sounds/id6584512882)
- An unofficial iOS port called "Engine Simulator" advertises a community section and a global leaderboard — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/engine-simulator/id6772016143)

### Inferences
- Demand ranking, combining upvotes and repeat requests:
  1. Easy in-app engine building (GUI configurator, live swap or hot reload).
  2. More engine architectures: turbo/supercharger, diesel, rotary, 2-stroke, VR/W.
  3. Vehicle and drivetrain tuning, plus performance readouts such as 0–60 and units.
  4. Audio export (WAV, loops, VST) for creators and game developers.
  5. Pops, bangs and backfire.
  6. Explanatory visuals.
- For a browser tool with deep customization, the top item maps directly onto the planned feature set. The value lies in making cylinder layout, firing order, cams and exhaust editable through a GUI rather than .mr scripts.
- The repeated "boring after 20 minutes" and "more engines" feedback on mobile apps suggests that goals (0–60 runs, leaderboards, challenges) and a large preset library help retention.

### Gaps
- GitHub Discussions on engine-sim could not be queried (GraphQL unavailable), so the most-upvoted discussions are unknown.
- YouTube comment themes were not retrievable; I found no reliable comment summaries.
- Google Play reviews were not retrieved. App store review counts come from aggregators and are small samples.

## 3. Common complaints

### Takeaway
The dominant complaints are about access and performance, not missing physics. They include Windows-only builds, crashes on start, high CPU load and lag even on strong PCs, hard .mr scripting with little documentation, community engines breaking between versions, awkward keyboard driving controls, and some audio artifacts.

- **Frustration over the missing GUI:** "WHY IS THIS SO UNFUNCTIONAL!!!!" (7 reactions) — [#349](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/349); "PLEASE FIX NO ENGINE BUG!!!" (9 reactions) — [#348](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/348).
- **Stalled development:** The original repo has not been pushed since 2024-06-17 — [GitHub](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim). GitHub search results describe the original as written to demo in a YouTube video rather than as a finished product (seen in a search snippet).

### Inferences
- A browser tool removes the top complaints by design: no OS lock-in, no install or DLLs, no "is this a virus" worry. Performance is the main risk to manage. The native C++ sim struggles even on high-end PCs, so a browser version needs scalable quality settings (sample rate, simulation frequency, cylinder count limits) and probably WebAssembly plus AudioWorklet.
- Version-stable, shareable engine files matter. Breaking community engines between versions caused visible frustration.
- Clear explanations of what each parameter does would ease the confusion between "it won't start" and "I made a mistake" (for example, warnings for bad firing order or cam timing).

### Gaps
- I could not quantify complaints in Steam reviews (Steam blocked) or YouTube comments.
- Issue keyword counts are rough matches, not hand-labelled categories, and they overlap.

## 4. Features that drive sharing and virality

### Takeaway
Viral content comes from three things: impossible or unusual engines (inline-11, V11, V24, 20,000 rpm singles, rotaries), faithful recreations of famous real engines (Chevy 454, Ferrari F136, B58, VQ37), and hearing a sound you could never hear in real life. Sharing works through engine files (catalog site, BeamNG mods) and YouTube or Shorts recordings, so recording, export and file sharing are what amplify reach.

### Cited Findings
- **Unusual engines get press coverage:** The Drive and Car Throttle covered a simulated inline-11 ("sounds like a better V10"), a 20,000 rpm single-cylinder, a simulated V11, and a rotary that "braps and stutters" — [The Drive, inline-11](https://thedrive.com/news/this-simulated-inline-11-cylinder-engine-sounds-like-a-better-v10); [Car Throttle, inline-11](https://www.carthrottle.com/post/this-is-what-an-inline-11-engine-would-sound-like); [Car Throttle, 20k rpm single](https://www.carthrottle.com/post/this-20000-rpm-one-cylinder-engine-simulation-sounds-bizarre); [The Drive, V11](https://www.thedrive.com/news/ever-wondered-what-a-v11-engine-sounds-like-well-here-you-go); [The Drive, rotary](https://www.thedrive.com/news/listen-to-this-rotary-engine-simulator-brap-and-stutter-just-like-the-real-thing)
- **Community parts catalog with extreme builds:** The engine-sim.parts catalog bills itself as a place to share and discover engines and components for the simulator. One entry is an "F1 V24 stable splitrod (unrealistic)" — [catalog.engine-sim.parts](https://catalog.engine-sim.parts/); [V24 part #1948](https://catalog.engine-sim.parts/parts/1948); [engine-sim.parts](https://www.engine-sim.parts/). A "V2 to V24 All Engine Sounds" comparison video appears on an aggregator page (title only, seen in search results).
- **Real-engine recreations:** BeamNG mod authors package Engine Simulator versions of real engines (B58, VQ37VHR, J35A8, EZ30D) — [BeamNG mod](https://www.beamng.com/resources/engine-simulator-engines-for-beamng-cars-b58-vq37vhr-j35a8-ez30d.31220). Users report issues from building the Ferrari F136 — [#378](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/378); the VW 1.6 TDI — [#457](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/457); the Audi I5 with VTEC — [#393](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/393). Users were frustrated that the Chevy 454 from the video was not shipped — [#13](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/13)
- **Tutorials and collab videos as a format:** Fillman86's "Inline 7 Cylinder!! ft. AngeTheGreat for a tutorial" — [recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/next-gen-engine-sim-inline-7-cylinder-ft-angethegreat-for-a-tutorial-by-fillman86)
- **Export as a sharing enabler:** Requests for WAV render and loops ([#49](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/49), [#415](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/415)) and for recording sound ([CE #81](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/81)).
- **Pops and bangs:** Explicit backfire requests exist ([#96](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/96), [#343](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/343)) but have little GitHub engagement.
- **Leaderboards and community in mobile ports:** An unofficial iOS "Engine Simulator" port advertises a community section and a global leaderboard — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/engine-simulator/id6772016143)

### Inferences
- Features likely to spread a browser simulator, ranked:
  1. Shareable link or code per engine build, so others can hear it in one click. This replaces downloading .mr files.
  2. One-click recording to MP4 or WAV with an on-screen tach, sized for 9:16 Shorts and TikTok.
  3. A preset gallery of famous engines plus a "weird engines" category.
  4. A/B sound comparison (for example, the same engine with two firing orders, or V8 cross-plane vs flat-plane).
  5. A pops-and-bangs or anti-lag toggle. It has few GitHub upvotes, but it fits the short-video "exhaust crackle" format (inference).
- A remix or fork flow on shared engines, plus a "most played" gallery, would rebuild the catalog's community loop on the web.

### Gaps
- No view counts for specific weird-engine videos or Shorts were retrievable, and I found no TikTok data.
- The catalog's popularity and download counts could not be read (host blocked).

## 5. Educational use

### Takeaway
I found no direct evidence that AngeTheGreat's engine-sim is used in formal teaching. Demand for learning features shows up only in a few requests (flow visualization, hover explanations) and in users who say they want to experiment with engine design. Web-based IC engine simulators for education do exist in the engineering-education literature, and the new browser tool Combustion Lab leans toward thermodynamics rather than sound.

### Cited Findings
- **Learning-oriented requests:** Air and exhaust flow visualization — [#82](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/82); hover text describing each GUI item, with images of what it changes — [#23](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/23). One user downloaded the sim "for engine testing, designing and experimentation purposes" — [CE #14](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/14). A request for a 0–60 page alongside the oscilloscope views — [CE #67](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/67)
- **Education framing around the Steam sim:** A YouTube video is titled "Steam Engine Simulator A Bit Of Fun Learning - It's time for SCIENCE" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kB0u35WFH_o)
- **Academic precedent:** An ASEE paper describes the development of a web-based IC engine simulator (WICES) — [ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/15036); [PDF](https://strategy.asee.org/development-of-a-web-based-ic-engine-simulator-wices.pdf). An SAE paper (2011-01-1109) covers simulation-based teaching of engine mapping and calibration — [SAE Mobilus](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/a-practical-simulation-based-approach-teaching-engine-mapping-calibration-fundamentals-2011-01-1109). NASA's EngineSim is a K-12 educational simulator for jet engines, not piston engines — [NASA GRC](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/K-12/airplane/test6.html). A MATLAB "enginesim" also exists — [MathWorks File Exchange](https://uk.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/55742-enginesim)
- **Browser rival with a thermodynamics focus:** Combustion Lab (combustionlab.net) reached about 235 points and 33 comments on Hacker News around July 2026, per a mirror snippet. Per write-ups, it runs in a browser tab with no install and no login. It uses a single-zone model solved per crank angle, with Wiebe combustion, Woschni heat transfer and Livengood-Wu knock prediction. A write-up contrasts it with engine-sim, a native C++ desktop app focused on sound synthesis — [HN mirror](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48795900); [youngju.dev](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/2026-07-11-web-combustion-engine-simulator.en); [zeli.app](https://zeli.app/story/48795900)
- **Search for classroom use:** A search for classroom use of engine simulators returned mainly marine engine-room training simulators (Kongsberg K-Sim). This shows simulator-based training exists in vocational settings, but not with consumer engine-sound tools — [Kongsberg K-Sim Engine](https://kongsberg.com/maritime/products/simulation/k-sim-engine/k-sim-engine-instructor)

### Inferences
- Education looks like a secondary segment that strengthens the main one rather than a primary market. Firing-order and crank-phase explainers, P-V and pressure-vs-crank-angle plots, and "why does a flat-plane V8 sound different" overlays would serve students and also give enthusiasts good material for shareable videos.
- Combustion Lab's HN reception (235 points) shows that a technical web audience values a physically grounded browser engine model. A tool that combines Combustion Lab-style plots with engine-sim-style sound would cover both audiences.

### Gaps
- I found no teacher testimonials, syllabi or course pages using engine-sim, and no survey of classroom demand for P-V diagrams.
- I could not confirm whether Combustion Lab produces audio (its pages and the HN thread were blocked).

## 6. Mobile vs desktop usage expectations for a browser tool

### Takeaway
There is steady, multi-year demand from users who have only a phone, Chromebook or Mac, ending with a 2026 request to "make it a game on chrome". Mobile users already have a market of sample-based engine sound apps whose reviews ask for more engines and customization. A browser tool must work on phones, but native engine-sim's CPU problems show that mobile needs reduced-fidelity modes.

### Cited Findings
- **Mobile, web and ChromeOS requests on GitHub, 2023–2026:** "will this be added for mobile" (2024, 13 comments) — [#447](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/447); an Android version, with the note that the Winlator emulator closes the app (2025) — [#458](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/458); "Brah just make it a game on chrome not a download... my phone can't unzip it" (2026-07-25) — [#468](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/468); "WebAssembly and browser support... would be awesome" (2023) — [#428](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/428); a question about running it on ChromeOS (2023) — [CE #40](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/40); the Godot port or web demo idea (8 reactions) — [#20](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/20)
- **Existing mobile engine sound apps:** These include Trans4motor, RevHeadz, XLR8, RealEngineSim, Potenza Drive (OBD2) and Motion Beats (phone sensors). Common critiques are limited customization, difficulty matching a real car, and boredom after a short session — [appfollow Trans4motor](https://appfollow.io/ios/trans4motor-engine-simulator/899277186?country=us); [justuseapp RevHeadz](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/793064343/revheadz-engine-sounds/reviews); [appshunter Potenza Drive](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/potenza-drive-engine-sounds/id6584512882); [App Store Motion Beats](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744122841). Another iOS app is "Engine Sim - Sound Simulator" — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/enjine-sim-engine-sound-sim/6787443044)
- **Unofficial mobile port of the engine-sim concept:** "Engine Simulator" on iOS (id6772016143) advertises a community section and a leaderboard — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/engine-simulator/id6772016143)
- **Desktop performance ceiling:** The native sim lags even on an i9 / RTX 2080 Ti ([#381](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/381)) and on mid-range GPUs ([#111](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/111), [CE #14](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/14)). Users asked for an offline WAV render so low-spec laptops can produce smooth audio — [#49](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/49)
- **Desktop power users want hardware input:** wheel, pedals and shifter ([#36](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/issues/36), [CE #64](https://github.com/Engine-Simulator/engine-sim-community-edition/issues/64)).
- **Browser precedent:** Combustion Lab runs in a browser tab with no install or login — [youngju.dev](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/2026-07-11-web-combustion-engine-simulator.en)

### Inferences
- Expect two modes. Mobile: quick play with presets, a touch throttle and shift paddles, shareable clips, and a lighter simulation. Desktop: the full editor, keyboard and gamepad or wheel input (the Gamepad API), and higher simulation rates.
- Mobile users are probably the youngest, most ad-exposed and most viral segment. Desktop users are probably the deep builders who create the engines everyone else plays.
- Graceful degradation (auto-adjusting simulation frequency, an offline "render this rev sweep" option) protects low-end phones and Chromebooks, which the GitHub requests show are a real share of interested users.

### Gaps
- I found no data on the mobile vs desktop traffic split for engine sound content, and no Web Audio performance benchmarks for real-time engine synthesis on phones.
- I could not open App Store or Google Play pages directly, so rating counts and review texts come from aggregators only.
